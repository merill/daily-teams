In Microsoft Teams, your users can record their Teams meetings, 1:1 calls, webinars, and town hallsevents to capture audio, video, and screen sharing activity. There are policies that control recording in each type. Many users use the term "meetings" and "calls" interchangeably.

Use this article to find the policy that controls recording in Teams meetings, webinars,meetings and town halls.events.

  1. Confirm the type: meeting, webinar, town hall,event, or call.
  2. Confirm the policy scope: meeting policy, events policy, or calling policy.
  3. Confirm who needs recording permission: the organizer, co-organizer, or presenter.

There are two recording features that can be enabled with policy:

Convenience recording- an: An ad-hoc recording of a call or meeting that a user starts and manages. For an overview of convenience recording, see Overview- Recording and transcription for Teams meetings and calls.

Compliance recording-calls: Calls and meetings that are automatically recorded without user intervention and owned by the company, using a third-party solution.

We recommend that you check your call recording policy settings as well. If the settings are different for meetings and calls, it might cause confusion for your users. To learn about managing recording for Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN calls), see Configure call recording, transcription, and captions in Teams.

  4. Open the case, and then on the Collections tab, select New collection.
  5. Commit the new collection.

Troubleshooting

If users can't record a meeting, first verify if it was a Teams meeting, 1:1 calls, webinar, or Town hall.event. Then verify the matching policy and recording setting assigned.
