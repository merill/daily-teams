Microsoft Teams: Malicious QR code detection in messages

10 November 2026MC1526651 on mc.merill.net ↗Message

In brief

Microsoft Teams will detect malicious QR codes in messages starting mid-November 2026, warning users of harmful links. Detection applies to chats and channels, with external QR codes blurred by default. The feature is enabled via existing Malicious URL protection, requiring no admin action.

Message Center announcement

Updated November 10, 2026: We have updated the timeline. Thank you for your patience. 

What and why

Microsoft Teams is extending malicious URL protection to provide near-real-time detection of malicious destinations embedded in QR codes shared in messages. Teams will analyze QR code destinations to identify potential phishing and fraud threats and display an additional warning when malicious content is detected.

Rollout schedule

  • General Availability (Worldwide): Beginning in mid-November 2026 (previously early November) and expected to complete by late November 2026 (previously mid-November)

Impact on your organization

Who is affected

  • Users who use Microsoft Teams messaging

Platforms and services

  • Microsoft Teams chats, meeting chats, and channels

What will happen

  • Teams will decode and evaluate QR code destinations in inline images shared in 1:1 chats, group chats, meeting chats, and channels.
  • Detection applies to internal and external communications.
  • Images containing QR codes from external senders are already blurred by default. When Teams identifies a malicious QR code, users will also see the warning, “This message contains a link that might be harmful,” and a “Learn about file and link safety” link.
  • QR code images from internal senders are not blurred by default. The same warning will appear when Teams detects a malicious QR code.
  • For users on Teams mobile in China, QR code images are blurred by default regardless of whether the sender is internal or external, and any messages containing malicious QR code will be blocked.
  • Malicious QR code detection is managed by the existing Malicious URL protection setting.
  • The setting is enabled by default. Organizations with Malicious URL protection enabled will automatically receive malicious QR code detection as the feature rolls out.
  • No separate configuration is available or required for malicious QR code detection.

Action required and recommendations

No admin action is required.

Admins can review or change the existing Malicious URL protection setting in Teams admin center > Settings and policies > Messaging Safety.

We recommend that you:

  • Inform users and help desk staff about the additional warning for detected malicious QR codes.
  • Clarify that QR code blurring is a separate precautionary experience and does not mean that Teams has identified a QR code as malicious.
  • Review internal documentation about Teams messaging security and update it if necessary.

No compliance considerations identified. Review as appropriate for your organization.
